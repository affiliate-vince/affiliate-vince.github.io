Vince's honest take

Perfect for
  • People who shoot action in places drones can't fly — trails, dunes, slopes, no-fly zones — and want hands-free follow footage.
  • Early-adopter types who accept a 3–6 month (or longer) delivery window and firmware growing pains in exchange for roughly 31–36% off the planned retail price.
Skip it if
  • You need a follow camera before this ships. A $4.2M campaign with this many units will realistically face production delays — don't back it for a trip next month.
  • You expect flawless software on day one. Auto-following and auto-editing are AI features that improve over time — first batches ship with the features you read about, but polish lands via updates.

Risk & reality check

The big one — overfunding. 8,400% funded means tens of thousands of units. Every extra unit is supply-chain and QC pressure. Expect the possibility of 3–6+ month delays beyond the stated estimate, and read the latest update for the current production timeline before deciding.

Hardware + software. Beni is a robot with AI features. Mechanical issues surface in the first production run; auto-follow polish happens via firmware. Backers are, in effect, early QA. That's the deal at 31–36% off — just go in knowing it.

What's solid. Funding is far beyond viable, the team communicates regularly (12 updates), and the feature set is grounded in proven camera-robot tech — this is not a vaporware poster.

The bottom line

Beni is a genuinely interesting ground robot with credible funding and an engaged team — but it's a hardware gamble with a likely delay baked in. If you're an early adopter with patience and a use case drones can't cover, the early-bird pricing is the reward for that risk. If you need footage next month, buy a drone.
